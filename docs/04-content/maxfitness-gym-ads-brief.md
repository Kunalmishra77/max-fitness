> **This advertises the gym to people who might join it.** V4You's own product advertising
> — selling the platform to gym owners — is `v4you-product-ads-brief.md`. Keep the two
> apart: different advertiser, different audience, different everything.
>
> Written on 2026-10-27 on a misreading of the brief. Kept because Ajay will want it
> eventually, and the facts in it were verified against the running system.

Everything an AI video tool needs about Max Fitness Gym, and everything it must not invent.

Companion to `hero-video-brief.md` (the website's hero clips). That one is mood, no words.
This one is an advertisement: it has to say something and ask for something.

---

## 0. Read this first — what AI can and cannot make

An AI video generator can produce a convincing gym. It cannot produce **this** gym, Ajay,
or the members who train here. That matters more than it sounds:

- A local gym ad works on recognition. Someone in Nyay Khand should think *"that's the
  place opposite Sai Mandir"*. A generated gym says *"this is a stock advert"*, and the
  people most likely to join are exactly the ones who will notice.
- Showing a building, equipment and staff that are not the gym's is a misrepresentation
  Meta can act on, and a member can be disappointed by on day one.

**So: film the gym on a phone, and use AI for what AI is good at** — the titles, the
transitions, the price card, the map end-card, and any abstract shots. Section 5 lists
exactly what to film; it is about twelve minutes of work.

If there is genuinely no footage yet, run the ad as a **still-image carousel** from the
photographs that already exist rather than a generated video. A real photograph beats a
synthetic film for a neighbourhood gym every time.

## 2. What must never appear in this ad

**Claims the gym cannot prove.**

> **No championship, medal or "national champion" line about the owner.** There is no
> certificate, event or year on file. The website deliberately replaced that story with
> what members actually write in their reviews, and the ad follows it. If the owner
> produces proof, it can go in — not before.

Members' own medals are different: real members, with their written consent, showing their
own achievement is fine.

**Meta's rules for health and fitness ads.** These get ads rejected and accounts limited,
and they are easy to break by accident:

| Do not | Why |
|---|---|
| Before-and-after photographs, or any split-screen body comparison | Banned outright |
| "Lose your belly", "fix your body", "are you overweight?" | Implies knowledge of a personal attribute |
| Close-ups of a stomach, thighs or any isolated body part | Reads as body-shaming |
| "Ideal body", "perfect physique", a number on a scale | Unrealistic outcome |
| Any person's image without their written consent | The gym's own rule, and the law's |

**Say it the allowed way instead:** talk about *training*, *coaching*, *starting*,
*strength*, *the first week*. Show people lifting, not bodies being judged.

## 3. The 60-second script

One idea: **"You don't have to know anything to start. Someone will show you."**

That is what this gym actually sells — a 26-year-old neighbourhood gym where the owner
coaches on the floor — and it is the fear that stops most first-timers walking in.

Hindi voice, English and Hindi on screen. Indirapuram's audience reads both; the voice
should sound local, not like a national brand.

## 4. AI prompts — one per shot, ready to paste

Use these for the shots you cannot film, and for every title and graphic. Each already
carries the brand's look so the cuts match.

**The house style, append to every prompt:**

> Cinematic, premium, calm. Deep blacks, slightly desaturated colour, natural skin tones,
> a touch of red rim light. Slow confident camera — dolly or gentle push-in, never shaky,
> no fast cuts, no flashing or strobing. Indian neighbourhood gym, daytime, warm practical
> lighting. 1080×1920 vertical, 30fps. No text, no logos, no watermarks, no subtitles
> burned in.

| Shot | Prompt |
|---|---|
| 1 | Extreme close-up, a hand placing a dumbbell onto a rack, knurled metal catching a red rim light, shallow depth of field, slow push-in. Dark gym background, out of focus. |
| 2 | Wide static shot of a modest Indian neighbourhood gym floor, a few people training, nobody looking at camera, mirrors along one wall, warm light from high windows, slightly desaturated. |
| 4a | Slow dolly along a row of treadmills in a small Indian gym, one person walking at an easy pace, morning light, calm and unintimidating. |
| 4b | Battle ropes on an open wooden floor, slow motion, ropes rising and falling, step platforms and agility cones at the edge of frame, red light low in the background. |
| 6 | Abstract motion-graphic background for a price card: deep black, soft red gradient from one corner, very slow drifting light, no objects, no text. Leave the centre clean and dark. |
| 8 | Map-pin animation: a minimal red location pin drops onto a dark stylised street map and pulses once. Black background, red accent, no labels, no place names, no text. |

**Titles and the price card: do not let the video model draw text.** Every model spells
badly in Devanagari and often in English. Generate the background with prompt 6, then add
the words in the editor — CapCut, Canva or Premiere — in the brand's own type.

**Voice.** An Indian Hindi voice, male or female, warm and unhurried, with English words
left in English (*gym*, *trainer*, *functional floor*). Speed around 0.95. Do not let a
generator pronounce "Nyay Khand" or "Kuliyal" unchecked — listen before you publish.

## 5. What to film yourself — about twelve minutes

Shoot vertical, 1080×1920, phone at chest height, no zoom. Clean the lens first; it is the
single biggest difference between footage that looks professional and footage that does not.

| | Shot | Length |
|---|---|---|
| ☐ | Ajay correcting a member's form — hands on the bar, actually coaching | 20 s |
| ☐ | A slow walk down the cardio row, phone steady at chest height | 15 s |
| ☐ | The functional floor — ropes, step platforms, cones | 15 s |
| ☐ | Two members training and talking, no one looking at the camera | 15 s |
| ☐ | Ajay to camera, one sentence in his own words — **not** a script | 15 s |
| ☐ | The entrance from the street, so people recognise the door | 10 s |

**Consent.** Written, from every person whose face appears, before the ad runs. One line in
a WhatsApp message is enough: *"मैं जिम के विज्ञापन में अपनी फ़ोटो/वीडियो इस्तेमाल करने की
अनुमति देता/देती हूँ।"* Keep the replies.

## 7. Campaign setup

| | |
|---|---|
| **Objective** | **Leads**, with WhatsApp as the destination — not Traffic. A gym this size needs conversations, not clicks, and WhatsApp is where this gym already answers. |
| **Location** | A **3 km radius** around the gym, not "Ghaziabad". Nobody drives across a city to a neighbourhood gym. Indirapuram, Vaishali and Vasundhara fall inside it. |
| **Age** | 20–45 |
| **Gender** | All. Run the women's price in its own ad set if you want it to land — otherwise one ad set is enough to start. |
| **Interests** | **None at first.** With a 3 km radius the audience is already small; adding interests starves delivery. Let Meta find them. |
| **Budget** | ₹300–500 a day for the first week. Below ₹200 Meta cannot learn; above ₹1,000 you are paying to discover what ₹400 would have told you. |
| **Placements** | Automatic. Deliver the vertical 9:16 cut and it fits Reels, Stories and Feed. |
| **Schedule** | All week. Most enquiries will come between 6–10 pm. |

**Before you spend anything:**

- The **Meta Pixel** on the website, and a **Lead** event on the sign-up confirmation.
  Without it you are guessing which ad worked, and Meta is guessing too.
- Decide **who answers the WhatsApp**, and how fast. A lead answered in five minutes and
  one answered the next morning are not the same lead.
- Every message will land in Max Register's **Messages** screen, so the desk can see the
  whole conversation beside the member's record.
